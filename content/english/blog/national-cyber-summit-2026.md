I went to National Cyber Summit with my dad. Full conference passes were nearly $1000 for the two of us, so we skipped the talks and spent the whole day in the exhibition hall.

This is a summary of what I got out of it and how I plan to use it.

## Who is actually in cybersecurity

The thing I noticed first was how many people came from a military background, or just stumbled into the field. Not many people seemed to follow a straight line from "I like computers" to a **civilian** security career
(I'm talking about private sector; a large amount was obviously government contracting).

That was a bit surprising for me, as at my age I already *think* I know what I want to do with my life, but many people seem to have just gotten into this field by the river of life.

## WolfSSL

WolfSSL was the booth that stood out most to me. They make all sorts of embedded technology, which especially matches well with me making cybersecurity tooling. Heck, I've already implemented Shamir's Secret Sharing and ChaCha20Poly1305.
When I'm older, I'll likely look into internships, and WolfSSL is very high on my admittedly tiny list.

## Lunch

I sat down with some people over lunch, and they gave me some advice that I'll be sure to make note of:

* Networking and internships matter a lot.
* Certifications are worth more than degrees, at least according to them.

It's one conversation, so I'll keep asking around, but it's good to know now.
It also lines up with what I already thought, so I think I know where I'll start to build my career.

## The community

I was surprised by how open everyone was. I already knew people in this space were nice from Open Source Summit this May, but I honestly expected a room full of professionals to be less willing to talk to a kid.
They were friendly, they answered questions, and nobody brushed me off as "just a kid".

## Takeaway

Overall, I learned a lot and had a very positive experience. You don't need the expensive pass to get something out of a conference. Walking the floor and talking to people works fine.
